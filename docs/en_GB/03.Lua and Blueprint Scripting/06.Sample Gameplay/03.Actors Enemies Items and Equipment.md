# Actors, Enemies, Items and Equipment

## Actors and characters

`Actor` supplies map ownership, position, lifecycle and collision-facing behaviour. Character-style classes add movement and direction. Public class defaults belong on the Lua class table; `init` is reserved for constructor arguments and private runtime state.

### Actors in a composite world

A `WorldGameMap` keeps one world-coordinate Actor registry. Crossing a ready child boundary rehomes the same instance; holes remain part of the world. Colliding Actors cannot enter unloaded placements, while non-colliding roots sleep and request them asynchronously.

Only roots in Active 32×32-cell chunks simulate or render. Leaving invokes `onWorldSleep()` and returning invokes `onWorldWake(elapsedSeconds)` without replacing `onCreate` or `onDestroy`. Never-activated Prepared roots receive no lifecycle and are absent from gameplay lookup.

Tile and Actor drawing follows the manifest `layerOrder`; runtime-only layers are appended in first-use order. The player remains last on its layer. Hiding a child Tile layer also hides that layer's Actors from colour and lighting-surface rendering.

`getActorByTag` searches Active Actors and resident roots that slept after activation without loading a child; evicted Actors remain absent until reload.

Map indexing and streaming traverse Actor trees with native root-first BFS, preserving child order. Repeated Actors and cycles are errors.

`SceneMap:recordDestroyedActorTag` suppresses that identity in resident and future child payloads without loading an absent child. A suppressed Actor cannot run lifecycle, become queryable or reappear after eviction. The Actor-taking `recordDestroyedActor` API remains record-only.

## Movement, pathfinding and animation

`GameMap` caches tile passability and dynamic Actor occupancy. Actor-list or terrain changes rebuild the relevant cache, ordinary movement updates one Actor's occupancy, and pathfinding reconciles Actors whose occupied cells changed before running A*. A* and actual movement apply the same Dir4 edge rule: every newly entered leading cell must allow departure from its source tile and entry through the opposite direction of its destination tile. Click movement reuses the direct A* result for ordinary goals and one-cell blocked targets. If the moving Actor's footprint prevents that direct result, it falls back to searching the target's adjacent cells. A colliding target remains the final attempted step so its collision interaction still runs. When the adjacent-cell fallback is used and the impassable goal has no collider, the player stops at the adjacent cell rather than bumping toward the goal. Movement towards an ordinary non-colliding Actor with an executable `onOverlap` stops beside it rather than entering its cell, while an explicitly clicked Teleporter enters the selected occupied cell so its overlap transfers the player.

World pathfinding crosses loaded child boundaries but treats unloaded children as unavailable without implicit I/O. Holes remain passable with nil top material.

Sparse-world readiness, passability, Dir4 and top-material queries are native. Prepared bounds gate movement; material queries keep separate readiness rules.

`GameMap.MapViewRect` defines the logical-screen map canvas and defaults to `sf.IntRect.new(192, 32, 416, 416)`. Its position places the canvas and its size sets both the clipped drawing area and the Camera view size. A map axis smaller than the canvas is centred within that axis; a larger axis is clamped to its scrollable range. Map rendering, pointer conversion, world/UI positioning, animations, lighting, fog and weather share this rectangle, and pointer input outside it is ignored.

`PathRouteState` owns a copy of its route and exposes a revision that changes with the route. The preview renderer refreshes its cached route only after that revision changes, reuses its rectangle shape, and trims the visible route when the player enters another map cell. Changing only an atlas frame's source position does not refresh Actor bounds when the texture-rectangle size stays the same; changing the frame size still refreshes cached bounds and occupied cells.

Domain, Flank and Blockade Effects grant `Gameplay.Movement.Hazard`. Both movement and `MovementDangerGrid` use `MovementSpecials.Preview`, which dispatches `Event.Movement.QueryHazard`; keep their spatial rules in that path. Domain uses Manhattan range, Flank requires enemies on opposite adjacent sides, Blockade affects adjacent cells, and positive contributions add. After obtaining the Enemy Book, `EnemyDamageHintLevel` selects no hints (`NONE`, `0`), enemy readouts (`BATTLE`, `1`, default), or enemy and map-cell readouts (`MAP`, `2`). Hints have no gameplay side effects.

Click routes exclude positive-danger cells even while hidden, except an explicitly clicked destination. Clicking a Domain or Blockade enemy removes only that target's contributions, including its side of a Flank; collision or overlap targets the player will not occupy ignore destination-cell danger.

Newly Active world chunks invalidate the danger cache. Full grids materialise only for map hints or click pathfinding; single-cell queries remain local. A danger revision invalidates movement only when it affects the remaining path or enterable enemy goal; invalid routes return to the last complete cell and replan.

## Enemies

An Enemy Blueprint selects an Enemy General Data member. `GeneralDataTypes.Create` copies it into an `EnemyAttributeSet`; `Battler` exposes that set as `attributes` and owns its `AbilitySystemComponent`. Enemy General Data has no member events: combat behaviour uses explicit Gameplay Abilities and Effects.

`MAXHP` is an enemy's virtual durability; enemies do not retain mutable `HP`. Player `HP` belongs to `PlayerAttributeSet`, starts at `1000` in Player General Data, and is not stored as a Blueprint attribute override.

Player HP Current is constrained to `0..MAXHP` after modifiers. Permanent MAXHP and LEVEL progression reacts to Base changes, so temporary Infinite Effects do not alter permanent growth.

Enemy `special` retains its integer, boolean and `Reborn` class-path values. Each entry becomes an Infinite `Special.<id>` Effect with a matching tag and focused ability. Combat specials modify combat Gameplay Events; movement specials use `Event.Movement.QueryHazard` and `Gameplay.Movement.Hazard`.

Every Enemy grants `Ability.Combat.MotaBattle`. Its side-effect-free calculation returns a damage breakdown and numeric `BattleResult` (`WIN`, `CANNOT_DAMAGE` or `LETHAL_COUNTER_DAMAGE`). Collision validates damage, Game Over, reward and State Effect Specs before `CommitResult(result)`; previews reuse the same calculation without mutation.

`drops` maps each Item Blueprint path to an `sf.Vector2i` offset. Valid drops spawn on the enemy's layer, are persisted as added Actors, and activate their General Data `onDrop` graph through `GeneralDataGraphAbility`. A duplicate placement tag skips that drop; an invalid path or non-Item Blueprint is an error.

`Enemy.DefeatShatterEffectEnabled` is a global class setting that defaults to `true`; it is not a Blueprint field and cannot be overridden per enemy. At defeat finalisation, the Sample immediately records the enemy as destroyed and calls `destroy()`, so the enemy no longer blocks movement and its cell is passable. When the setting is enabled and shaders are available, a visual snapshot remains for about 0.65 seconds while world-space logical-pixel fragments scatter radially outward from the centre. The snapshot is purely visual and has neither collision nor light blocking. Disabling the setting, or running without shader support, keeps the immediate-disappearance behaviour.

After a win and both attack animations, the Enemy Actor Blueprint `onDefeat` event runs. Its completion starts fixed settlement: apply game-variable changes, record and destroy the Enemy, spawn drops or Reborn, then apply rewards and States. Enemy General Data has no `onDefeat` graph.

An enemy with the `Reborn` special uses that special's non-empty string value as a Blueprint class path. After the original enemy is recorded and destroyed, the replacement is spawned on the same map cell and layer with a fresh map-placement tag, runs its normal `onCreate`, and is recorded as an added Actor. Map transfer and save/load therefore keep the original placement destroyed and restore the replacement exactly once. An invalid Reborn path is a required-data error.

`afterBattleVarChanges` stores `variableName = { operator, operand }` for declared `int` or `float` variables. Operators are `=`, `+`, `-`, `*`, `/`, `//`, `%` and `**`; the saved shape remains an object whose values are two-element arrays.

Defeat preparation validates the configured operations before combat damage. After `onDefeat`, settlement evaluates them again against current variables through fixed `Engine.Eval` expressions and fresh locals; stored operators are never executed as source.

Damage previews count only counterattacks made before the attacker lands the defeating hit. For defender maximum HP `H` and positive incoming damage per round `D`, the counterattack count is `max(ceil(H / D) - 1, 0)`.

When accumulated Domain, Flank or Blockade damage defeats the player, the Sample applies that damage immediately but enters the Game Over scene only after the longest concurrently started attack animation finishes, matching ordinary enemy-battle timing.

## Items

Item member graphs implement `onUse` and `onDrop` as explicitly activated `GeneralDataGraphAbility` instances, not Actor events. `onUse` targets the Player; `onDrop` targets the spawned Item Actor. Target-bound nodes therefore use `GameplayEventData.target`; Player State nodes belong in `onUse`.

Collision callbacks on the highest occupied tile layer run from the visually topmost Actor to the bottommost Actor. Within one layer, later Actor entries are drawn and triggered first. When several collectible Items occupy one cell, a blocked movement attempt therefore collects the visually topmost Item; its destruction ends the current collision dispatch, and the next Item is collected by a later movement attempt. Holding a direction may collect subsequent Items on later fixed updates without requiring a key release.

`NodeFunctions.Mota.CenterSymmetricTeleport`, `GoUpstairsSamePos` and `GoDownstairsSamePos` provide the reusable logic for CenterSymmetricTeleport, UpstairsTransportation and DownstairsTransportation. The adjacent-floor nodes follow the current region's map order and check the persisted target map at the player's current zero-based coordinates before changing maps. The centre-symmetric node targets `(width - 1 - x, height - 1 - y)` on the current map. Each node exposes `Success` and `Failed` execution branches; failure resolves `FLY_FAIL` and displays it as a common tip without removing the item. Connect only `Success` to `NodeFunctions.Player.RemoveItem` when configuring a consumable item graph.

## Equipment

Equipment `attrPlus` becomes a slot-sourced Infinite Effect. It is applied before the explicit `onEquip` graph and removed before `onUnequip`; slot names must match Class data.

States are Infinite Effects carrying `State.<id>`, stacks, modifiers and abilities. Weak subtracts 1 ATK/DEF per stack with a zero minimum; the HUD shows the Ability System Current value and the Weak stack count, so base 10 with two stacks appears as `8 (-2)`. Poisoned adds damage through `Event.Combat.ResolveIncomingDamage`. State graphs listen to `Event.Movement.Step` (`onWalk`) or `Event.State.Trigger.<id>` (explicit hook).

## Runtime references

Use General Data identifiers as stable keys. Runtime application keeps every string verbatim, so class, animation and equipment-slot keys never depend on the selected language. Display names and descriptions may be brace-wrapped localisation identifiers; resolve them with `LOC` only when presenting them and resolve them again after a language change. The lookup table is managed by [Game Localisation Workflow](<../../05.Plug-in Development and Installation/06.Game Localisation Workflow.md>). Renaming an identifier requires updating every reference.

The exact Sample schemas and member lists are in General Data Reference. Practical Bottle, Gem, door and enemy examples are in Usage Recipes.

## Limitations

General Data identifiers are project contracts and are not repaired automatically when renamed. The Sample's combat and inventory rules are examples, not mandatory engine behaviour.
