# Actors, Enemies, Items and Equipment

## Actors and characters

`Actor` supplies map ownership, position, lifecycle and collision-facing behaviour. Character-style classes add movement and direction. Public class defaults belong on the Lua class table; `init` is reserved for constructor arguments and private runtime state.

## Movement, pathfinding and animation

`GameMap` caches tile passability and dynamic Actor occupancy. Actor-list or terrain changes rebuild the relevant cache, ordinary movement updates one Actor's occupancy, and pathfinding reconciles Actors whose occupied cells changed before running A*. Click movement reuses the direct A* result for ordinary goals and one-cell blocked targets. If the moving Actor's footprint prevents that direct result, it falls back to searching the target's adjacent cells. A colliding target remains the final attempted step so its collision interaction still runs. Movement towards an ordinary non-colliding Actor with an executable `onOverlap` stops beside it rather than entering its cell, while an explicitly clicked Teleporter enters the selected occupied cell so its overlap transfers the player.

`PathRouteState` owns a copy of its route and exposes a revision that changes with the route. The preview renderer refreshes its cached route only after that revision changes, reuses its rectangle shape, and trims the visible route when the player enters another map cell. Changing only an atlas frame's source position does not refresh Actor bounds when the texture-rectangle size stays the same; changing the frame size still refreshes cached bounds and occupied cells.

The live map also caches movement-special danger cells. Domain uses Manhattan distance strictly below its configured range, Flank requires the matching enemies on both opposite sides, and Blockade applies beside its enemy. Positive contributions on the same cell are added. `EnemyDamageText.EnemyDamageHintLevel` selects the expected-damage hints after the player obtains the Enemy Book: `EnemyDamageText.DamageHintLevel.NONE` (`0`) hides both per-enemy expected battle readouts and movement-danger cell totals, `BATTLE` (`1`, the default) shows only each enemy's readout, and `MAP` (`2`) additionally shows the total expected damage centred in every affected cell. The Enemy Book remains a prerequisite at every visible level. Cell hints reuse the enemy readout style and damage colours without a coloured background or border. This preview never executes Blueprint, overlap, walk, retreat, animation, or damage side effects. The level affects hints only, not HP loss or the actual-damage particle shown after a hit. Ordinary click routes always pass positive-damage cells to A* as excluded actor anchors, even before the Enemy Book reveals them. A clicked danger cell is allowed only as the explicit destination. Clicking any occupied cell of a Domain or Blockade enemy removes that target enemy's contributions from every candidate cell, including only its side of a Flank pair, while damage from every other source remains excluded. A collision or overlap destination that the player never occupies is likewise not rejected for danger on the destination itself. If the danger revision changes during click movement, the player returns to its last fully entered map cell and replans instead of rounding into the next cell; no safe route means no further movement.

## Enemies

Enemy Blueprints combine class defaults with an Enemy General Data member. Runtime data supplies combat values, rewards, drops, animation and special-effect configuration. The `onDefeat` graph is optional and is executed only when present.

`afterBattleVarChanges` defaults to an empty dictionary and stores each post-defeat update as `variableName = { operator, operand }`. Adding an entry first selects an unused declared `int` or `float` game variable. Its operator defaults to `=` and is chosen from `=`, `+`, `-`, `*`, `/`, `//`, `%` and `**`; its operand defaults to `0` or `0.0` and uses the selected variable's integer or floating-point editor. Changing between integer and floating-point variables resets the operand to that type's zero value. These types constrain editor authoring only: `GameInstance` does not enforce game-variable types at runtime, and the saved value remains an object key with a two-element array.

Damage previews count only counterattacks made before the attacker lands the defeating hit. For defender maximum HP `H` and positive incoming damage per round `D`, the counterattack count is `max(ceil(H / D) - 1, 0)`.

When accumulated Domain, Flank or Blockade damage defeats the player, the Sample applies that damage immediately but enters the Game Over scene only after the longest concurrently started attack animation finishes, matching ordinary enemy-battle timing.

## Items

Item records define presentation, usability, price, icon and whether use consumes the item. Member graphs implement `onUse` and `onGet`. Keep reusable algorithms in Lua or Common Functions rather than duplicating large member graphs.

## Equipment

Equipment records define slot and attribute additions. `onEquip` and `onUnequip` graphs apply special behaviour around the ordinary stat changes. Slot names must match the selected Class data.

## Runtime references

Use General Data identifiers as stable keys. Runtime application keeps every string verbatim, so class, animation and equipment-slot keys never depend on the selected language. Display names and descriptions may be brace-wrapped localisation identifiers; resolve them with `LOC` only when presenting them and resolve them again after a language change. The lookup table is managed by Plug-in Development and Installation. Renaming an identifier requires updating every reference.

The exact Sample schemas and member lists are in General Data Reference. Practical Bottle, Gem, door and enemy examples are in Usage Recipes.

## Verification

Create one actor of each relevant kind and verify its Blueprint defaults, referenced General Data record and member graphs. Click a free cell, a colliding target, a non-colliding `onOverlap` target, and each occupied cell of one-cell and multi-cell Teleporters, then repeat after moving or spawning a blocker. Test all three damage-hint levels before and after obtaining the Enemy Book, lethal Domain, Flank and Blockade damage with different animation durations, and animations with differently sized frames. Test equipment equip and unequip and consumable use and removal independently.

## Limitations

General Data identifiers are project contracts and are not repaired automatically when renamed. The Sample's combat and inventory rules are examples, not mandatory engine behaviour.
